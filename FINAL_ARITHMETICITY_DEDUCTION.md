# From persistent local expansion to arithmeticity

This note spells out the two final deductions, including the applications of
Takeuchi's criterion and Thurston's rigidity theorem.

## Algebraic character

Let \(\Pi\) be the marked surface group, let
\(\rho_x:\Pi\to\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf R)\) be a lift of the Fuchsian
representation, and let \(\Gamma\) be its projective image. Put
\[
 \Delta=\rho_x(\Pi^{(2)}),\qquad
 \Lambda=\Gamma^{(2)}<\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbf R).
\]
Thus \(\Lambda\) is the projective image of \(\Delta\). Let

\[
 k_x=\mathbf Q(\operatorname {tr}_x b:b\in\Pi^{(2)})
\]

be the ordinary trace field of \(\Lambda\). The squared-trace identity
identifies it with the invariant trace field of \(\Gamma\). For every
nonidentity \(b\in\Pi^{(2)}\), the
persistent local-expansion theorem with \(y=x\) gives

\[
 \mathcal J_{k_x}(\operatorname {tr}_x b)
 \le {\ell_x(b)\over2}.
\tag{1}
\]

The distinguished real place is one of the nonnegative summands defining
\(\mathcal J_{k_x}\), and its value is exactly \(\ell_x(b)/2\). Hence
equality holds in (1), and every other local summand vanishes.

At every finite place \(v\), vanishing means
\(|\operatorname {tr}_x b|_v\le1\). Thus every trace of \(\Delta\) is an
algebraic integer. The full inverse image of \(\Lambda\) consists of
\(\pm\rho_x(b)\), so its traces are the sign-stable set
\(\{\pm\operatorname {tr}_x b:b\in\Pi^{(2)}\}\). At every
nondistinguished archimedean place, vanishing
means

\[
 \sigma(\operatorname {tr}_x b)\in[-2,2].
\tag{2}
\]

In particular all images of the trace generators are real, so the full
trace field \(k_x\) is totally real. The three facts now established are:

1. \(k_x\) is totally real;
2. every trace in the full inverse image of \(\Lambda\) is an algebraic
   integer;
3. for each nondistinguished embedding
   \(\sigma:k_x\hookrightarrow\mathbf R\), all traces in that full inverse
   image satisfy (2).

These are the trace hypotheses of the finite-covolume corollary of
Takeuchi's criterion, applied to the ordinary trace field of \(\Lambda\),
which is the invariant trace field of \(\Gamma\). The criterion also
requires finite covolume.
That condition holds because the subgroup generated by squares has finite
index: a finitely generated group modulo its subgroup generated by squares
is a finite elementary abelian \(2\)-group. Apply Takeuchi to this
finite-covolume subgroup and then use that arithmeticity is invariant under
commensurability.

The central traces \(\pm2\) are integral and lie in \([-2,2]\) under every
embedding, so they require no separate argument.

## Transcendental character

Assume now that the distinguished Fuchsian character \(x\) is
transcendental. Its rational closure

\[
 Z_x=V(I(x)),\qquad
 I(x)=\{f\in\mathbf Q[\mathcal X]:f(x)=0\},
\]

has positive dimension. Algebraic specialization supplies distinct
algebraic Fuchsian points

\[
 y_j\in Z_x(\mathbf R),\qquad y_j\longrightarrow x.
\tag{3}
\]

The real local inverse branch used in the specialization construction can be
restricted to the Fuchsian component containing \(x\), so every \(y_j\) lies
in the same marked Teichmüller space. All
squared-trace collisions at \(x\) persist at each \(y_j\). Fix one
\(y=y_j\). For every \(\gamma\in\Pi\), apply the persistent local-expansion
theorem to \(b=\gamma^2\). The distinguished real place at \(y\) is a
nonnegative summand of the local-expansion height, so

\[
 \ell_y(\gamma)
 ={\ell_y(\gamma^2)\over2}
 \le \mathcal J_{k_y}(\operatorname {tr}_y(\gamma^2))
 \le {\ell_x(\gamma^2)\over2}
 =\ell_x(\gamma).
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the marked length spectrum at \(y\) is termwise no larger than the one
at \(x\). Thurston's minimal-stretch theorem identifies the logarithm of the
least Lipschitz constant in the marked homotopy class with the supremum of
these logarithmic length ratios. The resulting asymmetric metric is
nonnegative and separates points of Teichmüller space. Equation (4) makes
this distance at most zero, hence zero, and therefore \(y=x\). This is
impossible because \(y\) is algebraic and \(x\) is transcendental.

Thus the distinguished character must be algebraic, and the first part
proves arithmeticity.

## From the critical exponent to Schmutz and Sarnak

The focused theorem assumes only

\[
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}
 {\log\#(T(\Gamma)\cap[-X,X])\over\log X}\le1,
\]

equivalently \(\#(T(\Gamma)\cap[-X,X])=O_\eta(X^{1+\eta})\) for every
\(\eta>0\). Literal linear growth is a special case.

If every interval of a fixed positive length contains at most \(M\) signed
trace values, then \([-X,X]\) can be covered by \(O(X)\) such intervals.
Hence the signed trace set has \(O(X)\) elements of absolute value at most
\(X\). The compact bounded-clustering conclusion therefore follows from
the compact linear-growth conclusion.

For a cocompact Fuchsian group with torsion, Selberg's lemma gives a
torsion-free finite-index subgroup. Its trace set is a subset of the original
trace set, so every growth hypothesis above passes to it. Arithmeticity of
the subgroup, and hence of the original group, follows by commensurability.

## External results used in the deduction

The focused manuscript states Takeuchi's criterion with finite covolume
explicit, proves the invariant trace-field identities used for the square
subgroup, and cites Thurston's minimal-stretch theorem in its separating
asymmetric-metric form and Selberg's lemma for the orbifold extension.
Finite generation by squared-trace coordinates is tied to the chosen affine
character model.

# Provenance and influence ledger

## Purpose and scope

This ledger records where the important notions in this repository first
entered the project and what influence they had. It is a provenance record,
not an attribution or novelty claim. In particular:

- **Present in Bogachev** means that the notion or argument is explicitly
  present in Nikolay Bogachev's initial manuscript,
  [Proof of the Positive Trace Gap Conjecture](paper/positive-trace-gap.pdf)
  ([TeX source](paper/positive-trace-gap.tex)), whether or not it was already
  standard in the literature.
- **Session development** means that the formulation, generalization, or use
  first appeared during the research session represented by this repository.
  It does not mean that the underlying mathematics is new to the literature.
- **External input** means a result imported from the cited literature.
- **Transformed seed** means that the initial manuscript contains a genuine
  precursor, but the session changed its role or mathematical form
  substantially.

The influence labels are:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | A logical dependency of the focused Schmutz--Sarnak proof. |
| **Structural** | Used directly in the focused manuscript as framework or background, rather than as a new proof step. |
| **Diagnostic** | Changed the direction of the research by exposing an obstruction or eliminating a route. |
| **Catalytic** | Generated later ideas but is not used in the focused proof. |
| **Parallel** | Produced results in the long companion but is not needed for the focused theorem. |
| **Verification** | Improves confidence, exposition, or bookkeeping without supplying a mathematical implication. |

The focused proof's own logical dependency graph is in
[paper/compact-trace-rigidity.tex](paper/compact-trace-rigidity.tex),
Section 10. The longer exploratory record is
[paper/trace-gap-companion.tex](paper/trace-gap-companion.tex).

## Clarifications about similarly named ideas

### Fricke extension versus Fricke identity

The **Fricke extension** is Bogachev's arithmetic example
\(2\mathbf Z\cup\sqrt2\,\mathbf Z\). The **Fricke commutator identity**
\[
 \operatorname{tr}[A,H]
 =\operatorname{tr}(A)^2+\operatorname{tr}(H)^2
  +\operatorname{tr}(AH)^2
  -\operatorname{tr}(A)\operatorname{tr}(H)\operatorname{tr}(AH)-2
\]
is a standard identity recruited during the session to close the degenerate
branch of the moving two-trace fiber theorem. They have no logical
connection beyond the shared historical name.

### Raw components versus trace colours

Bogachev's manuscript contains the raw two-component set
\(2\mathbf Z\cup\sqrt2\,\mathbf Z\), but it does not introduce a general
notion of trace colours or a grading by a finite quotient.

The following were introduced during the session relative to the initial
manuscript:

- trace colours indexed by sign or Kummer data;
- the grading by \(\Gamma/\Gamma^{(2)}\);
- Galois sign characters;
- Kummer-graded trace rings and finite integral Kummer trace algebras;
- mixed energy between colour components;
- scalar colour countermodels.

These notions explained why individually separated components can have dense
cross-differences. They were **diagnostic and exploratory**, not critical:
the focused proof contains no colour or Kummer formalism.

### The original recurrence and its use in the final proof

The elementary trace-of-powers recurrence is already in Bogachev's
Proposition 3.7. The following additions, developed during the session, are
needed for the final proof:

1. the shifted polynomials \(q_n\);
2. the matrix identity
   \(A^n=q_n(\operatorname{tr}A)A-q_{n-1}(\operatorname{tr}A)I\);
3. its two positive and negative twisted-trace projections;
4. Cassini coprimality of consecutive terms;
5. the use of \(q_{N-1}/q_N\) in Roth's theorem;
6. the use of \(q_{N-1}q_N\) as the modulus in the length-two-path
   congruence argument.

Thus the recurrence itself is inherited. Its use to define the Roth divisor
and the congruence modulus was developed during the session.

## Counterfactual significance for the actual research trajectory

The preceding influence labels say what an idea did. This section answers the
stronger question posed about the session trace: was the idea essential for
the model to find or maintain the successful strategy?

This counterfactual assessment cannot be as objective as logical dependence,
so “essential” has a deliberately operational meaning. An idea is called
essential when, in the actual recorded trajectory, it either supplied the
first productive mathematical germ, forced a necessary change of direction,
or is an unavoidable step of the final proof. It does not mean that no
mathematician could ever find another proof without it.

| Code | Counterfactual meaning |
|---|---|
| **EG: essential germ** | Started the productive line of investigation in a mathematically specific way. |
| **EP: essential pivot** | Closed a misleading route or opened the route that became successful. |
| **CP: critical proof step** | Logically indispensable to the focused proof as written. |
| **HA: helpful accelerator** | Improved understanding or shortened the search, but was not necessary to reach or prove the result. |
| **NP: nonessential/parallel** | Mathematically useful, but neither needed for the successful strategy nor used in the final proof. |
| **VO: verification only** | Checked or clarified the result after the mathematical strategy existed. |

An entry may have two codes: for example, the recurrence was a strategic
pivot and its transformed version is also a critical proof step.

### External results

For the proof as written, simultaneous Roth, bounded-power
superapproximation, strong approximation, Brooks--Burger transfer,
Gorodnik--Nevo counting, Lang--Weil, Noether normalization, Northcott--Kronecker,
Thurston's minimal-stretch theorem, and Takeuchi are all **CP**: each supplies
a necessary stated implication.
They were not essential germs of the session, however. Their strategic role
was to provide the implications required after the strategy of fixing the
recurrence index and varying the group element had been identified.

## Net influence on the final compact proof

The final proof has three provenance layers.

1. **Inherited structural layer.** Signed traces, the subgroup generated by
   squares, the invariant trace field/quaternion algebra, the
   trace-of-powers recurrence, quaternion orders, and trace--length
   conversion are already present in Bogachev's manuscript, although most
   are standard background.
2. **Bogachev-specific catalytic layer.** The explicit \(R(g)\) argument
   showed that two trace coordinates can linearize a group operation. The
   Fricke extension then showed that this argument cannot detect
   bounded clustering. These two ideas strongly shaped the search but are
   absent from the final dependency graph.
3. **Proof developed during the session.** Persistent collision loci,
   algebraic Fuchsian specialization, the local expansion functional, the
   Roth--Chebyshev divisor, the prime-square sieve for the varying elements,
   uniform two-trace fibers, the length-two-path congruence inequality,
   cancellation at the critical exponent, and the Takeuchi--Thurston
   conclusion form the proof given in the focused manuscript.

Accordingly, the simplest accurate summary is:

> The initial manuscript supplied the problem, the principal algebraic germ,
> the square-subgroup arithmetic framework, and the obstruction that forced a
> change of method. The remaining proof was developed during the session. No
> Bogachev-specific theorem is a logical input to the focused
> Schmutz--Sarnak proof, but several notions already present in his manuscript
> remain part of its structural vocabulary.

## Maintenance rule

When a new result is added to the repository, record:

1. the first document in this project in which the idea appears;
2. whether the session merely reformulated it or changed its mathematical
   use;
3. whether it is critical, structural, diagnostic, catalytic, parallel, or
   verification-only;
4. the exact theorem or section that depends on it;
5. whether the entry is a provenance statement only or also has a separate
   literature-priority audit.

This prevents three recurrent confusions: an idea can be important without
being used in the final proof; a session formulation can be new to the
project without being new to mathematics; and a standard idea present in the
initial manuscript can be inherited without being specific to that
manuscript.

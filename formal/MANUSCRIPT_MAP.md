# Focused-manuscript-to-Lean correspondence

This file maps the Lean project to `paper/compact-trace-rigidity.tex`, the
focused manuscript.  It does **not** claim to formalize
`paper/trace-gap-companion.tex` or Bogachev's
`paper/positive-trace-gap.tex`.

The Lean project has two parts:

1. kernel-checked proofs of several algebraic, combinatorial, and limiting
   calculations; and
2. a kernel-checked implication from explicit mathematical assumptions to the
   torsion-free compact-surface parts of the Schmutz and Sarnak conclusions.

Each mathematical assumption is a field of a Lean structure, supplied when a
theorem is invoked rather than declared as a Lean axiom.  Some fields state a
single elementary fact from the manuscript.  Others state conclusions that the
manuscript derives from several external theorems.  These are labelled
**proved in the manuscript and assumed by Lean** below.  They are not presented
as verbatim statements from the literature.

## Kernel-checked calculations

| Manuscript calculation | Lean declaration | What is checked |
|---|---|---|
| Chebyshev recurrence and Cassini identity | `q_cassini`, `q_consecutive_bezout` | Polynomial identities over every commutative ring |
| Positive and negative scalar recurrence projections | `traceRec_succ_eq`, `negativeTraceRec_succ_succ`, `two_trace_projections` | Scalar recurrence identities; the Cayley--Hamilton realization as matrix traces is outside the model |
| Quadratic elimination for the fibers of \((\operatorname{tr}g,\operatorname{tr}(a_mg))\) | `moving_fiber_quadratic_identity` | Direct elimination from two determinant-one equations and the trace equation |
| Fricke identity and factorization by the commutator trace | `fricke_trace_identity`, `moving_fiber_fricke_factorization`, `moving_fiber_rhs_eq_commutator` | Polynomial identities over every commutative ring |
| Chinese-remainder restriction for the length-two-path map | `wedge_middle_congruent`, `wedge_triple_eq_of_same_middle` | A Bezout relation for `P,Q` confines equal-image middle coordinates modulo `PQ`; over a domain, the image and middle coordinate determine the whole path |
| Counting length-two paths by residue classes | `principalResidueClass`, `wedge_fiber_card_le_principal_residue_class`, `congruence_wedge_bound_from_residue_classes` | Defines the finite class modulo `PQ`, derives the fiber bound from its maximum population, and proves `|E|² ≤ |V| M |X₋| |X₊|` |
| Abstract finite-fiber counting | `card_le_fiberBound_mul_product`, `square_sum_degrees_le`, `abstract_congruence_wedge_bound` | Reusable factorization of the same finite-fiber and Cauchy--Schwarz argument |
| Limit as the parameter `m` tends to infinity | `MovingLimitWitness`, `moving_parameter_limit` | Removal of the normalized `O(1)` and `o(m)` terms from the assumed eventual estimate |
| Order of the four remaining limits | `half_bound_from_manuscript_order`, `half_bound_from_full_manuscript_estimate` | Fix `N`, choose `0 < ε < 2N-4`, choose `θ` and the auxiliary data, take `m → ∞`, then `θ → 0`, `ε → 0`, and `N → ∞`; the resulting coefficient is exactly `1/2` |

These formal proofs are independent of the final conditional theorem.  The
recurrence, fiber, Fricke, and length-two-path declarations are not used as
premises of that theorem, and Lean does not yet derive its analytic
assumptions from them.

underlying analytic estimates.

## External results behind the statements assumed by Lean

The external mathematics is proved in the cited literature, not in Lean.
The focused manuscript states the specialized form it uses and derives the
statement subsequently assumed by Lean.  The last column records the logical
direction.  “Normalized equivalence” permits only the displayed changes of
notation, absolute-value normalization, or affine/projective coordinates.
“Implies” means that the cited theorem is stronger or is combined with
additional deductions in the manuscript; no converse is claimed.

The fields `movingEstimate`, `specializationRigidity`, and

longer part of the final specialization statement represented in Lean.

## Consistency example and counterexample

`TraceSparsity/ContractAudit.lean` supplies a finite example with nonempty
types of characters and elements, distinct central and noncentral elements,
and simultaneous values of `OrderedLocalExpansionInputs` and
`ArithmeticInputs`.
It also applies the ordered implication in that example.  This shows that all
dependent assumptions can be satisfied simultaneously; it does not realize
them by a hyperbolic surface.

The same file constructs a second example satisfying `CriticalTraceGrowth`
but not `Arithmetic`.  The growth predicate therefore does not imply
arithmeticity without the local and arithmetic assumptions, especially
`movingEstimate`, `takeuchi`, and `finiteIndexAscent`.

## Verification boundary

The project checks the internal deductions in the focused proof.  It does not
formalize hyperbolic surfaces, character varieties, number fields, Roth's
theorem, superapproximation, spectral transfer, lattice counting, Thurston's
theorem, Northcott's theorem, or Takeuchi's criterion.  The finite example
shows only that the abstract assumptions are logically consistent; the project
does not supply those assumptions for a concrete surface in Lean.
The final Selberg finite-index passage from the torsion-free theorem to the
orbifold corollary is likewise outside the formal model.

Assuming the statements and hypotheses listed above, Lean's kernel checks the
internal algebra, quantifier order, limiting coefficient, equality argument,
use of Takeuchi's criterion, finite-index ascent, and final implications.  The
table separately records the correspondence between the TeX and Lean
statements and whether each cited result is used through a normalized
equivalence or a one-way implication.
The release scan must contain no project-specific `axiom`, `sorry`, or
`admit`; `#print axioms` may report only the documented standard logical
principles inherited from Mathlib.

## Target implication

Let \(\Pi\) be a closed surface group and let \(x\) be a faithful cocompact
Fuchsian character.  Its rational closure is
\[
  Z_x=V(I(x)),\qquad
  I(x)=\{f\in \mathbf Q[\mathcal X]:f(x)=0\}.
\]
The central theorem to be proved is the following.

> **Persistent local-expansion theorem.**  Assume that the signed trace set
> at \(x\) has linear growth.  Let \(y\in Z_x\) be an algebraic faithful
> cocompact Fuchsian character, let \(k_y\) be the full trace field of the
> subgroup generated by squares, and let
> \(b\in\widetilde{\Pi^{(2)}}\setminus\{1\}\).  Then
> \[
>   \mathcal J_{k_y}(\operatorname {tr}_y b)
>       \le \frac{\ell_x(b)}2.
> \]

The intended deductions are:

1. If \(x\) is algebraic, take \(y=x\).  The distinguished real place
   already contributes \(\ell_x(b)/2\); hence every other local contribution
   vanishes.  The resulting integrality and archimedean bounds are exactly
   the hypotheses of Takeuchi's arithmeticity criterion.
2. If \(x\) is transcendental, bounded-degree algebraic points
   \(y_j\in Z_x\) approaching \(x\), the local-expansion theorem, finitely
   many squared-trace character coordinates, and Northcott give a
   contradiction.
3. Bounded clustering implies linear trace growth, so the compact Sarnak
   conclusion follows from the compact Schmutz conclusion.

The scope of this historical worksheet was the torsion-free cocompact
Fuchsian case.  It did not claim the corresponding noncompact or orbifold
statements; the postscript above records the later orbifold extension.

## Dependency graph

The local-expansion theorem is obtained from

* the reduced-divisor form of Roth's theorem for
  \(q_{N-1}/q_N\);
* a sieve producing elements
  \(a_m=b^m h_m\), \(|h_m|\leq \theta m\), for which all valuations of
  \(q_{N-1}(\operatorname {tr}a_m)q_N(\operatorname {tr}a_m)\) are bounded;
* a uniform subexponential bound for the fibers of
  \(g\mapsto(\operatorname {tr}g,\operatorname {tr}(a_mg))\);
* a congruence-counting theorem for fixed powers of square-free rational
  moduli, followed by the elementary congruence inequality obtained from
  length-two paths in the trace graph; and
* the ordered limit
  \(m\to\infty\), then \(\theta\to0\), then \(\epsilon\to0\), then
  \(N\to\infty\).

No Kummer-root, inner/outer-form, additive-energy, arbitrary-ideal,
pointwise-radical, or Wieferich statement is a dependency.

## First-audit issues already isolated

1. The phrase “property \(\tau\) and uniform lattice counting” is not an
   adequate substitute for the combined congruence estimate. The new
   manuscript must state a theorem with its quotient, metric ball, modulus
   range, uniformity in the residue class, and error term.
2. The local functional changes by a degree factor under enlargement of the
   containing field.  Every occurrence of \(\mathcal J_k\) must specify the
   field, and base change must never be treated as equality without the
   factor.
3. The algebraic point degree, the degree of a chosen lifted representation,
   and the degree of the full invariant trace field are different notions.
   Their comparison needs a lemma rather than prose.
4. In the proof of the two-trace fiber bound, a zero value of the quadratic norm is not a
   divisor problem.  It is handled separately through
   \(\operatorname {tr}[A,H]=2\), absence of parabolics, and cyclic
   centralizers.
5. The final theorem uses lengths at \(x\) and arithmetic data at \(y\).
   Every ball, displacement, and trace must carry its subscript until the
   specialization \(y=x\) is made.
6. The transcendental case needs only finitely many character coordinates,
   but the reason their values lie in uniformly bounded-degree fields must be
   written before Northcott is invoked.
7. The phrase \(\log N\mathfrak c=O(R)\) is too weak for congruence
   counting.  The manuscript must display
   \(3\log N\mathfrak c<\delta_D R-o(R)\) (with the harmless adjustment if
   the selected group model has a different fixed dimension) and choose the
   ball-radius constant only after \(D\), \(A_D\), and \(\delta_D\).

## Scope of the Lean verification

The Lean development checks the internal chain of implications.
It will prove recurrence identities, polynomial identities, finite graph
inequalities, the coefficient squeeze, and the assembly of the final
implications.  Roth, expansion, congruence counting, specialization,
Northcott, and Takeuchi will occur as explicit Lean hypotheses. A clean
formal build therefore means “the conclusion follows from these displayed
inputs,” not “the external inputs have been formalized.”

Completion requires all of the following:

* the focused LaTeX source compiles without unresolved references;
* every row above is closed by a proof or an exact external theorem;
* a language/structure audit is recorded after the first mathematical audit;
* a second mathematical audit is recorded after all expository edits;
* the Lean project builds with no `sorry` and its assumptions are printed and
   compared with the statements cited from the literature.

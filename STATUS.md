| Uniform expansion over complete discretely valued fields | Complete | At every divisor with unbounded adjoint image, every direction expands by \(c\lambda^n\) somewhere in the word ball of radius \(n\). |
| Trace-slab volume asymptotics | Complete Haar-volume calculation | Gives \(X\) when \(\det A\ne0\), and \(X\log X\) when \(\operatorname{rank}A=1\); explicitly not a lattice-point theorem. |
| Noetherian locus preserving all squared-trace equalities at a fixed character | Complete | Reduction theorem; classification of its positive-dimensional components remains open. |
| Two-evaluation trace-fiber theorem | Complete | For two evaluations \(A_0,A_1\) of the same left factor, with one common representation of \(H\), the condition \(\operatorname{Tr}^2(A_1A_0^{-1})\ne4\) gives subexponential simultaneous fibers. If every collision for \(A_0\) persists for \(A_1\), every \(A_0\)-fiber is subexponential. The second evaluation may be non-Fuchsian. |
| Divisorial trace-entropy bound obtained by varying a multiplier | Complete | For the generic representation on a locus preserving all squared-trace equalities at a fixed character, or for an algebraic Fuchsian representation, a loxodromic \(k\in\ker\phi\) at a divisor gives \(h_{\rm Tr}(x)\ge\delta_J/(1+\ell_x(k)\mathfrak c_v/\ell_v(k))\). The proof converts one collision among the elements \(ak^nh\) into two independent trace coordinates and applies the two-evaluation theorem. |
| Loxodromic kernel element at every unbounded divisor | Complete | Zariski density and normality show that an unbounded local representation has an element of positive local translation length in \(\ker\phi\). Thus the remaining issue at such a divisor is the stated inequality controlling cancellation, rather than the existence of the required element. |
| Pure cyclic twist coefficient theorem | Complete | Equality of one Laurent squared trace along a pure semisimple twist fixes the two extreme coefficients and leaves at most two projective matrices. Persistent fibers are therefore uniformly bounded, giving trace entropy at least \(\delta_H\). |
| Dimension obstruction from jets of a nonconstant family | Complete | For a nonconstant Zariski-dense family of representations, every jet closure is the full \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) jet group. The \(r+1\) coefficients of one trace series leave fiber dimension at least \(2(r+1)\), and an explicit Laurent family realizes this loss of two thirds of the dimension. Counting coefficients of one trace series therefore cannot, by itself, produce two independent functions of the remaining matrix variable. |
| Failure of separate pigeonhole estimates for two trace coordinates | Complete | At trace entropy \(1/2\), separate pigeonhole arguments for two trace functions guarantee a common-fiber exponent of at most \(\delta_J-1\le0\), even after passing to covers. The model \(x+ty\) on \(\mathbb F_q^2\) is sharp: every scalar function has large fibers, while every independent pair has singleton fibers. |
| Limitation of adding further Cartan powers | Complete | A word involving arbitrarily many Cartan powers still provides at most four linear coordinates of a matrix. Here \(C_{\mathcal L}(R)\) is the largest logarithmic ratio between the values of two selected linear coordinates on differences of matrices from the radius-\(R\) set. Extracting \(q\) coordinates uniformly by comparing valuations requires coefficient gaps with total size greater than \((q-1)C_{\mathcal L}(R)\), in addition to the displacement cost from noncommensurable factors. Thus adding Cartan factors does not, in general, reduce the cancellation loss. |
| Restriction rigidity on loci preserving all trace equalities | Complete | An irreducible positive-dimensional locus preserving all collisions at a Fuchsian point cannot keep fixed a complementary subgroup \(H\) with \(\delta_H>1/2\). On every such locus through the point, restriction to Hao's \(H\) is birational onto its image. |
| Infinitesimal restriction rigidity | Complete | On every reduced subvariety through the Fuchsian point on which all of its squared-trace equalities continue to hold, restriction to any complementary convex-cocompact \(H<\ker\phi\) with \(\delta_H>1/2\) has injective differential. This excludes nonintegrable vertical tangent directions as well as vertical families, and gives \(\dim T_xZ\le 3\operatorname{rank}(H)-3\). |
| Algebraic restriction-field and quaternion-algebra rigidity | Complete | If \(x\) is algebraic and \(h_{\rm Tr}(x)<\delta_H\), the full adjoint trace field is generated by squared traces on the complementary convex-cocompact subgroup \(H<\ker\phi\). Moreover \(H^{(2)}\) already generates the full invariant quaternion algebra over that field. No nontrivial field embedding or proper invariant subalgebra can be invisible on \(H\). |
| Finite-cover amplification of trace equalities | Complete | In every genus-\(G\) cover, at least \(c e^{(\delta_H-1/2)R}\) globally distinct squared-trace functions coincide on the rational closure; along covers the exponent can approach \(1/2\). This is a reduction, not yet a contradiction. |
| Simultaneous Chebyshev-recurrence control | Complete | Dependent random choice on the two-trace graph gives one set of \(e^{(1/2-o(1))R}\) values satisfying \(\#(W_R+\beta_nW_R)\le e^{n\ell+o(R)}\#W_R\) simultaneously for every recurrence index \(n\). |
| Determinants of finitely many traces \(\operatorname{Tr}(a^jg)\) | Complete | For any finite set of exponents \(J\), the maximal minors of the coefficient matrix for \((\operatorname{Tr}(a^jg))_{j\in J}\) generate exactly \((q_{\gcd(i-j)}(\operatorname{Tr}a))\). Consequently, recording finitely many such traces does not produce a product of independent recurrence moduli. |
| First noncommuting recurrence determinant | Complete | For an irreducible pair \(a,b\), the four coordinates attached to \(I,a^m,b^n,a^mb^n\) have determinant equal to a fixed nonzero factor times \(q_m(\operatorname{Tr}a)^2q_n(\operatorname{Tr}b)^2\). Thus the two recurrence moduli are independent. However, an incidence family drawn from one ball has only \(e^R\), rather than \(e^{2R}\), elements. |
| Chebyshev CRT uncertainty on curves | Complete | Pairwise coprime recurrence indices have disjoint zero divisors. Their joint reduction gives \(\#U\le\prod_n\#(U+\beta_nU)/\#U\) once the total zero degree exceeds the common pole degree, and yields the exact entropy--pole-degree inequality. Function-field \(abc\) is already saturated and gives no stricter leading coefficient. |
| Recurrence criterion from congruence counts of length-two paths | Complete implication theorem | Expected trace occupancy modulo \((q_{n-1}(\operatorname{Tr}a)q_n(\operatorname{Tr}a))\) forces \(\mathcal J_k(\operatorname{Tr}a)=\ell(a)/2\). Applied to every element over the full trace field, Takeuchi gives arithmeticity. |
| Full restriction-of-scalars closure | Complete | The full invariant trace field excludes diagonal blocks between distinct embeddings, so the connected \(\mathbb Q\)-Zariski closure is \(\operatorname{Res}_{k/\mathbb Q}\mathrm{SL}_1(B)\); this justifies the bounded-exponent expansion input after restriction of scalars. |
| Bounded-degree algebraic specialization | Complete | Every positive-dimensional rational closure through a Fuchsian point contains algebraic Fuchsian points of uniformly bounded degree converging to it. All collisions at the original point persist, so trace support on balls for the original metric can only shrink. |
| Congruence criterion using balls for the original metric | Complete | At an algebraic specialization, congruence counting on balls defined by the original metric gives \(2\mathcal J_{k_y}(\operatorname{Tr}_y a)\le\ell_x(a)\). Applied to squares, this bounds the specialized marked length of every closed curve by its original marked length. Thurston's asymmetric metric then identifies the specialization with the original point and rules out a transcendental original character. |
| Arbitrary-ideal superapproximation \(\Rightarrow\) compact Schmutz/Sarnak | Complete conditional implication | A uniform spectral gap for all prime-to-\(S\) ideals in every relevant \(S\)-integral restriction-of-scalars norm-one group implies both compact conjectures, including transcendental characters. That general superapproximation theorem is not currently known. |
| One-term Chebyshev radical upper bound | Complete, unconditional | Expansion for the square of a square-free rational modulus, the minimum-degree part of dependent random choice, and a residue-class injection prove \(\limsup n^{-1}\log N\operatorname{rad}_S(q_n(\operatorname{Tr}a))\le\ell(a)/2\) for every hyperbolic \(a\) in an algebraic Fuchsian group whose trace set has linear growth. This controls each individual recurrence term, not an average of consecutive terms. |
| Criterion using almost all residue classes modulo a full prime-power modulus | Complete implication and deficit theorem | If the trace set supplied by dependent random choice occupies \(N(q_n(s)^2)e^{-o(n)}\) classes modulo the principal ideal \((q_n(s)^2)\), then Takeuchi's local equality follows. Conversely, expansion at any nondistinguished place forces the proportion of occupied residue classes to be exponentially small. This is narrower than expansion modulo arbitrary ideals but remains unproved for unbounded prime-power exponents. |
| Fixed-metric one-term radical bound | Complete, unconditional | At every fixed algebraic Fuchsian specialization in the rational closure, the same argument on balls for the original metric gives \(\limsup n^{-1}\log N\operatorname{rad}_{S_y}(q_n(\operatorname{Tr}_y a))\le\ell_x(a)/2\). Constants may depend on the specialization; no uniformity is needed. |
| High-prime-power concentration | Complete, unconditional | Known bounded-exponent superapproximation implies \(\limsup n^{-1}\log N\mathfrak c_{n,D}\le\ell(a)\). If a nondistinguished place contributes a positive local expansion exponent, then at least \((2\mathcal J_k(s)-\ell(a)-o(1))n\) of the logarithmic norm lies above every fixed exponent cutoff \(D\). |
| Bounded-power localization for varying elements | Complete, unconditional | The exact density of \(\mathfrak p^e\mid q_m(\operatorname{Tr}g)\) in a good local \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) quotient is \(O_m((N\mathfrak p)^{-e})\). For fixed \(D\), truncate each excess valuation \((v_{\mathfrak p}-1)_+\) at \(D-1\). Fixed-power expansion bounds the average weighted sum of these truncated valuations and, after choosing \(D\), makes the corresponding untruncated sum arbitrarily small relative to the ball radius below a suitable exponential prime cutoff. The result averages group elements, not distinct traces, and explicitly leaves a large-prime interval uncontrolled. |
| Reduced-divisor Roth bound | Complete | Simultaneous-place Roth applied to a rational function with simple zero and pole divisors gives the prime-to-\(S\) coefficient \(2d-2-\epsilon\). For \(q_{N-1}/q_N\) this is \(2N-4-\epsilon\). This is a focused consequence of Bombieri--Gubler, Theorem 6.4.1, not a novelty claim. |
| Recurrence ideals of bounded prime-power depth for a varying element | Complete, unconditional | Fix \(N\). A short perturbation \(a_m=b^mh_m\) can be chosen so that it retains the sum of the local expansion rates of \(b^m\). Avoidance at finitely many fixed primes, followed by a sieve using expansion modulo rational \(p^2\), ensures that every prime exponent in \(q_{N-1}(\operatorname{Tr}a_m)q_N(\operatorname{Tr}a_m)\) is bounded independently of \(m\). |
| Uniform fibers for the two traces \(\operatorname{Tr}(a_mg)\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}g\) | Complete | An explicit quadratic norm identity bounds the fibers of \(g\mapsto(\operatorname{Tr}(a_mg),\operatorname{Tr}g)\) by \(\exp(o(m))\), uniformly when the complexity of \(a_m\) is \(O(m)\). When that norm is zero, the identity gives a commutator of trace two; cocompactness then reduces the count to a cyclic centralizer. |
| Congruence inequality from length-two paths in the trace graph | Complete, unconditional | Because the recurrence ideal has bounded prime exponents, fixed-power superapproximation applies after rational saturation. Pruning the trace graph and mapping its length-two paths to two residue-class invariants gives \(\log N\mathfrak c_m\le(2N-1)D_x(a_m)/2+o(m)\). |
| Local expansion bound at every algebraic Fuchsian point in the rational closure | Complete, unconditional under critical trace growth | Roth gives the lower coefficient for \(\log N\mathfrak c_m\), while the preceding length-two-path argument gives the upper coefficient. Taking \(m\to\infty\), then \(\theta,\epsilon\to0\), and finally \(N\to\infty\), yields \(\mathcal J_{k_y}(\operatorname{Tr}_y b)\le\ell_x(b)/2\) for every algebraic faithful Fuchsian specialization in the rational closure. The proof assumes only that the upper exponent of signed-trace growth is at most one, rather than the stronger estimate \(O(X)\). This is the general theorem from which the compact result follows; its novelty should not be asserted without independent expert review. |
| Source of large prime-power divisors in the recurrence | Complete, unconditional | Outside finitely many primes, \(v_{\mathfrak p}(q_n)=w_{\mathfrak p}+v_{\mathfrak p}(n/r_{\mathfrak p})\); the latter term has only \(O(\log n)\) total norm. Thus any exponential contribution from large prime powers must come from unusually large initial multiplicities \(w_{\mathfrak p}\). |
| One-term Chebyshev radical hypothesis \(\Rightarrow\) compact Schmutz/Sarnak | Complete conditional implication | The matching lower rate \(\limsup n^{-1}\log N\operatorname{rad}_S(q_n(s))\ge\mathcal J_k(s)\), combined with the proved upper rate, gives Takeuchi arithmeticity. The fixed-metric specialization and Thurston length-domination argument include transcendental characters. |
| Mersenne example showing the strength of the radical hypothesis | Complete | For \(k=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), \(s=3/\sqrt2\), the one-term radical hypothesis is exactly \(\limsup n^{-1}\log\operatorname{rad}(2^n-1)=\log2\). It implies infinitely many non-Wieferich primes to base \(2\), so the general pointwise hypothesis goes beyond what is currently known unconditionally for Lucas sequences. |
| Number-field \(abc\Rightarrow\) compact Schmutz/Sarnak | Complete conditional theorem | Number-field \(abc\) makes each recurrence term asymptotically square-free in norm. Combining this with the proved one-term upper bound, which uses expansion for the fixed square of a square-free rational modulus, proves both compact conjectures in full. No \(abc\) or spectral constants uniform across specializations are used. |
| Prime-power marginal estimates \(\Rightarrow\) mixed-modulus flatness | False without independence | The diagonal in \(\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_p\) has perfectly flat coordinate marginals but occupies only \(p\) of \(p^2\) joint classes. Separate local estimates cannot simply be multiplied. |
| Uniform \(<1/2\) fiber bound on arbitrary nonconstant loci | False | Arithmetic covers with two disjoint one-holed tori give nonconstant restriction fibers on which \(e^{(1/2-\eta)R}\) globally distinct primitive trace functions become one constant. Any successful bound must use the full collection of trace equalities on the rational closure or force the moving representation to be visible. |
| Adelic separation bound | Complete under the stated finite-place/escape hypotheses | Standard product-formula technology; gives an upper bound, not an equality. |
| Naive bounded clustering \(\Rightarrow\) approximate-ring extraction | False | Refuted by \(\mathbb Z\cup\sqrt2\mathbb Z\), even with coherent two-color scalar identities. |
| Arbitrary mixed recurrence-modulus expansion | Open stronger statement | It gives an independent proof using a fixed group element. Existing theorems cover bounded prime exponents and special split products, but not all ideals for a general \(S\)-integral twisted \(\operatorname{Res}_{k/\mathbb Q}\mathrm{SL}_1(B)\). It is not needed for the proof that varies the group element while fixing the recurrence index. |
| Full one-term Chebyshev radical lower rate | Open; implied by number-field \(abc\) | Combined with the proved upper bound it gives an independent stronger route. Universally it contains the Mersenne radical/non-Wieferich problem; the unconditional compact theorem bypasses this pointwise barrier through a group family. |
| Subexponential recurrence powerful part | Open; implied by number-field \(abc\) | It would still suffice to prove that the Lucas--Wieferich excess of \(q_n(s)\), or of two consecutive terms, is \(o(n)\) along an unbounded sequence. This is now a secondary formulation of the sharper one-term radical gap. |
| Trace sparsity \(\Rightarrow\) divisorial and archimedean boundedness | Open alternative | Descent on a proper model removes the separate algebraicity problem. The unconditional theorem obtained by varying the group element does not require this additional valuative step. |
| Remaining cancellation inequality at an unbounded nondistinguished place | Open alternative route | The divisorial trace-entropy theorem rules out rapid escape. The remaining inequality is still a valid formulation of the problem, but it is not needed for the shorter proof in the focused manuscript. |
| Control of every close trace difference | Open alternative | Still sufficient, but the exact fixed-trace additive-representation theorem shows that it cannot follow from ordinary same-fiber Freiman extraction. |
| Critical signed-trace growth theorem | Complete, unconditional in the focused manuscript | Every cocompact Fuchsian lattice whose signed trace-counting function has upper polynomial exponent at most one is arithmetic. The proof is sharp at exponent one for the trace-graph method and includes torsion by Selberg's lemma and commensurability. This strengthens both named compact conjectures at the level of hypotheses and requires independent expert verification. |
| Compact Sarnak bounded-clustering conjecture | Complete, unconditional in the focused manuscript | Bounded clustering implies linear growth and hence the critical exponent bound; the moving-element local expansion theorem, algebraic specialization, Thurston rigidity, and Takeuchi prove arithmeticity. This would settle the compact case of Sarnak's conjecture and requires independent expert verification. |
| Compact Schmutz linear-growth conjecture | Complete, unconditional in the focused manuscript | Linear signed-trace growth is a special case of the critical exponent theorem. The nonuniform case was previously proved by Hao; the compact claim here requires independent expert verification. |

## Corrections incorporated from the exploratory note

- The difference-set constants are \(8^{1-n}\) and
  \(8^{1-n/2}\), without the stray comma present in one draft formula.
- The root obstruction does not require conjugation equivariance.
- In the complex higher-rank order argument, coordinates lie in a fixed
  fractional \(\mathcal O_K\)-ideal, not necessarily an integer denominator.
- Containment of generic elements in the central simple algebra is propagated
  to every lattice element with the two-open-set trick; it is not a consequence
  of Zariski closure of that countable algebra.
- The fixed-element outer witness is first stated for the finite-index rational
  unitary subgroup, where \(g^*=g^{-1}\) is justified.
- The adelic exponent controls clustering from above; no equality is asserted.
- An arbitrary finite separated coloring supplied by bounded clustering has no
  arithmetic content.  Compatibility with \(\Gamma/\Gamma^{(2)}\), Fricke
  multiplication, and a common Galois model is essential.

Consistency of the formal hypotheses
====================================

The principal Lean theorem is conditional.  Two elementary tests clarify what
its assumptions say: they can all hold simultaneously as logical data, and
the formal critical-growth predicate by itself does not imply arithmeticity.

A finite consistency example
----------------------------

``TraceSparsity/ContractAudit.lean`` constructs a finite datum with

* one character and two abstract elements;
* a designated central element and a designated nonidentity element;
* identically zero length and local height;
* witnesses satisfying the ordered hypotheses used in the successive limits;
  and
* values of both ``OrderedLocalExpansionInputs`` and ``ArithmeticInputs``.

It then invokes the public ordered theorem on that datum.  This makes three
purely logical facts machine-checkable: the structures are not contradictory,
their dependent fields fit together, and the public theorem can actually be
applied.  The model is deliberately finite and carries no claim of being a
surface group.

Why critical growth alone is insufficient
------------------------------------------

A second finite datum sets ``CriticalTraceGrowth`` to true and ``Arithmetic``
to false.  Lean proves both facts.  Hence there cannot be a theorem of type

.. math::

   \texttt{CriticalTraceGrowth}\longrightarrow\texttt{Arithmetic}

from ``CompactTraceDatum`` alone.  Arithmeticity becomes derivable only after
the local and arithmetic hypotheses are supplied.  These include the
finite-covolume form of Takeuchi's criterion and the passage through a
finite-index subgroup.  This is where the mathematics proved in the
manuscript enters the Lean deduction.  In Lean, these results appear as
explicit fields of the input structures rather than as global axioms.

Interpretation for hyperbolic surfaces
--------------------------------------

Logical satisfiability is weaker than geometric realization.  For the
intended model, the abstract carriers and predicates are read as follows:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 32 68

   * - Abstract object
     - Intended surface interpretation
   * - ``Character``
     - Faithful cocompact Fuchsian characters in the selected real
       neighborhood
   * - ``IsPersistent``
     - Membership in the rational closure as used through persistence of
       squared-trace collisions
   * - ``IsAlgebraic``
     - Algebraicity of the character coordinates
   * - ``Element``
     - Elements of the subgroup generated by squares
   * - ``IsCentral`` / ``IsNonidentity``
     - The separately treated identity or central case and the nonidentity
       hyperbolic case
   * - ``localHeight``
     - :math:`\mathcal J_{k_y}(\operatorname{Tr}_y b)` at eligible algebraic
       characters

The table in ``formal/MANUSCRIPT_MAP.md`` compares this interpretation field
by field. The comparison is necessarily mathematical rather than formal: the
Lean project does not define character varieties, number fields, or Fuchsian
groups.
